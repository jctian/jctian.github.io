abstract: >-
    This paper investigates how lower‑mantle thermal conductivity affects the thermal‑chemical evolution of Earth’s mantle and core, motivated by significant uncertainty in bridgmanite’s thermal conductivity from recent mineral physics experiments. Our findings indicate a self-regulation of CMB heat flux with different lower mantle thermal conductivity due to the thermal and compositional feedback: higher thermal conductivity results in faster core cooling, reducing the temperature drop across the CMB thermal boundary layer, and facilitates the formation of larger basaltic piles above the CMB. This self-regulation results in a similar CMB heat flux across models with different thermal conductivities, which differs from the prediction from parameterised models.
